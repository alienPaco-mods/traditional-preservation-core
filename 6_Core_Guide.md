## 1. What This Mod Does

**Traditional Preservation (Core)**, TP Core for short, is the mod every Traditional Preservation mod needs: Salt, Fish & Meat, Yeast, Vinegar and Dairy. It carries the rules they share, so they all agree about the weather, the cellar and the time you were away.

It also adds the series' shared stations:

- the **Stone Hearth**, a knee-high stone firebox with an iron door and a stone-lined hole in its top: a stove, an oven and a fire to warm the stations round it (chapter 3);
- the **Forged Cauldron**, 60 L of forged iron you hang over the Stone Hearth's hole to heat whatever you pour into it (chapter 4);
- the **Smokehouse**, a plank shed the Stone Hearth's smoke can be led into (chapter 6).

On its own it adds only those, and the cauldron's recipe; it changes no vanilla recipe, item or loot.

## 2. Quick Start

> Enable **Traditional Preservation (Core)** with any Traditional Preservation mod. It loads first.

| Step | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Build a **Stone Hearth** (Build menu, Masonry) | 8 Clay Bricks, 2 Clay, a mason's trowel (kept). Masonry 2 | A fireplace that cooks and warms. 8 Masonry XP |
| **Add Fuel** and **Light** it | Firewood and a lighter, as any fireplace | Fire, room heat, an oven, a hob over the hole |
| **Forge Cauldron** at a forge | 2 Steel Sheets, 3 Charcoal, 1 Wire; a ball-peen hammer, pliers or tongs and a metalworking chisel (kept). Blacksmith 4, and the recipe (chapter 4) | A **Forged Cauldron**. 35 Blacksmithing XP |
| **Fit Cauldron** (the Stone Hearth's menu, fire out) | The Forged Cauldron, 2 Iron Pipes, 1 Heavy Chain, 1 Large Hook; pliers (kept) | The **Stone Hearth (Cauldron)**: 60 L over the fire. 5 Blacksmithing XP |
| **Pour into the Cauldron** | Any one fluid, up to 60 L | The cauldron heating towards 95 C |
| Wait | About 1.5 hours for 10 L | The cauldron **steaming** at 85 C |
| **Fill from the Cauldron** | Empty containers | Your fluid back, hot |
| With Fish & Meat: **Put Fat in the Cauldron** | Raw fat from butchering | Lard (Melted) or Tallow after an hour of steaming; it never burns here |

## 3. The Stone Hearth

A knee-high block of grey dressed stone in courses, under one large flat slab that overhangs it a little all round. An **iron door** closes the fire mouth on the side it faces, with three draught holes low down and two slits under its arch; in the middle of the slab, a small round hole lined with stones. It has two facings.

> *Margin note:* a stone stove with a hole in its top is about the oldest kitchen there is. The pot sits down in the hole, half in the fire, and the stone round it bakes whatever is shut in behind the door: hob and oven from one armful of wood.

To the game it is a fireplace, so everything a fireplace does, it does: add fuel, light it, put it out, cook in it, warm the room. Food inside cooks as in an oven, and a pot over the hole as on a hob.

**The hole shows what's cooking.**

| In the Stone Hearth | The hole shows |
|---|---|
| Nothing | Open: firelight down in it, smoke rising out of it while it burns |
| A stew, a soup, a stir-fry, an omelette, pasta, rice or any container holding a fluid (stove food) | A dark forged pot set in the hole, its lid on, steam curling round the rim |
| Only oven food (a pie, bread, a roast) | A sooted steel lid with a flat bar handle; the smoke leaks out round the door |
| Joined to a Smokehouse | The lid (or the pot, for stove food): the smoke goes up the flue |

Right-click it. Its **Stone Hearth** submenu has:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Stone Hearth Info** | A small window: the fire, the fuel, a note. **Clicking the Stone Hearth opens it too** |
| **Bank the Fire** / **Stoke the Fire** | While the fire is lit; also a button beside Put out Fire in the loot window. See chapter 5 |
| **Fit Cauldron** | Hangs the cauldron over the hole (chapter 4) |
| **Connect the Stone Hearth** | With a Smokehouse beside it: lays the flue (chapter 6) |

**Keep the door clear.** The tile in front of the Stone Hearth's door must be free: it won't build or go down with a crate, a fence, a wall on that edge or another station there, and nothing of the Traditional Preservation mods can be built on that tile afterwards. Picked up, it turns between its two facings as you put it down.

**You can't pick a Stone Hearth up** while it burns (it's too hot), while a cauldron is fitted, or while it's joined to a Smokehouse.

## 4. The Forged Cauldron

A round-bellied kettle of dark forged iron, greased and blacked, sooted below its shoulder. Fitted, it hangs by its bail from a Large Hook in the middle of a Heavy Chain, slung between two Iron Pipes set in the stone either side of the hole.

> *Margin note:* this is the cheese kettle of the Alpine huts, where a cauldron of 100 L and more swung on a crane over an open fire. Those were copper; a farm far from a coppersmith beat its own from iron sheet, greased it and kept it black.

**Forging it.** At a vanilla forge, as the forged cooking pot is made, scaled up: **Forge Cauldron** takes 2 Steel Sheets, 3 Charcoal and 1 Wire, with a ball-peen hammer, metalworking pliers or tongs and a metalworking chisel kept; Blacksmith 4. You learn it from the magazine *Everyday Smithing - May 1993* (the one that teaches the forged cooking pot), or at Blacksmith 5 with Cooking 2, or by researching a cauldron you've found.

**Fit and Remove.** With the fire out, the Stone Hearth's menu has **Fit Cauldron**: the Forged Cauldron, 2 Iron Pipes, 1 Heavy Chain and 1 Large Hook, with pliers kept; about three quarters of a minute, 5 Blacksmithing XP. It becomes the **Stone Hearth (Cauldron)**. **Remove Cauldron** takes it down again in half the time and gives every piece back; not while the fire burns, and not while the cauldron holds anything. A Smokehouse joined to the Stone Hearth stays joined: the two go together.

Fitted, the Stone Hearth still cooks and warms as before. Its submenu, now **Stone Hearth (Cauldron)**, adds:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Cauldron: 10 / 60 L Cow's Milk (Fresh), 62 C* | A grey status line: what the cauldron holds and how hot it is |
| **Pour into the Cauldron** | From any container on you or nearby with a fluid in it |
| **Fill from the Cauldron** | Into empty containers, or ones holding the same fluid with room left |
| **Empty the Cauldron** | Pours away everything in it at once: for rotten milk, or a fluid you're done with. Longer the more there is |
| **Remove Cauldron** | Above |

**Stone Hearth Info** then shows:

| Line | Reads |
|---|---|
| Fire | Lit, Banked or Out |
| Fuel | A bar (full is 12 hours of wood) and the time it will burn |
| Cauldron | A bar in the colour of what it holds, and the litres, of 60 |
| Holds | The fluid, or each fluid of a blend |
| Heat | Its temperature |
| Steaming | "Yes", "in 1.2 hours", "Not yet (85 C)" or "Not while banked" |

The window updates while it's open, and closes when you walk more than 6 squares away. What the cauldron holds shows in its mouth, in the fluid's own colour.

- **60 L, one fluid at a time.** Any fluid: water, milk, whatever a container holds. A mixture is refused, and so is a fluid that doesn't go with what's already in the cauldron. Both show in red, with the reason.
- **Some fluids may share it.** A mod can say which: Dairy lets cow's, sheep's and animal's milk mix, and the blend keeps its shares in and out.
- **Heat mixes by volume.** Cold poured onto hot cools the lot.
- **Lit:** it heats towards 95 C. Milk and the rest are kept off the boil.
- **Water boils.** Water on its own comes to a rolling boil at 100 C. Tainted water held there for **10 minutes** comes out clean: a whole cauldron of rain-barrel water made safe at once. If the fire goes out or you pour more in, the 10 minutes start over.
- **At 85 C it steams.** You see it in the cauldron, and in Stone Hearth Info, and you hear it: a gentle simmer at the Stone Hearth for as long as it stays that hot.
- **Fire out:** it cools towards the temperature of the place the Stone Hearth stands in. Slowly: the stone holds its heat.
- **More takes longer**, both ways.

| In the cauldron | From 20 C to the steam, fire lit | From 85 C down to 45 C, fire out, 20 C room |
|---|---|---|
| 5 L | about 1¼ hours | about 1½ hours |
| 10 L | about 1½ hours | about 2 hours |
| 20 L | about 2 hours | about 2½ hours |
| 60 L (full) | about 2¾ hours | about 3½ hours |

| Water | From 20 C to clean (boiling, then 10 minutes) |
|---|---|
| 10 L | about 1¾ hours |
| 20 L | about 2¼ hours |
| 60 L (full) | about 3¼ hours |

Water steams sooner than milk: nobody holds it back from the boil.

> *Margin note:* six times the fluid doesn't take six times the wait. A bigger potful has less surface for its size, so the time grows with the cube root of the volume: a full 60 L cauldron takes less than twice as long as 10 L.

What you fill from the cauldron leaves at the cauldron's heat. An ordinary container in your hands is back at room heat within seconds, as vanilla has it. Some mods keep the heat for you: Dairy's milk remembers it, and its Wrapped Yogurt Pot holds it for hours.

**While you're away** the cauldron carries on: it heats for as long as the fuel it had would have lasted, then cools.

## 5. Banking the Fire

**Bank the Fire** rakes a lit fire down under its own ash. The flames go out and the door's vents and the hole show dull embers.

| | Lit | Banked |
|---|---|---|
| Wood burnt | all of it | **a third** |
| The same fuel lasts | 1× | **3×** |
| The cauldron | heats towards 95 C | settles at about **50 C** |
| It cooks | at full speed | a third as fast |
| Warms the room | 8 tiles round | only the **2 tiles** round it |
| Light | the whole room | a faint glow |
| Stations within 2 tiles (chapter 7) | 28 C indoors | 24 C indoors |

**Stoke the Fire** rakes the embers back up into a full fire.

The embers keep burning while you're away, at their third. **Put out Fire** works on a banked fire too, and a banked fire that burns down goes out. Either way it isn't banked any more: light it again and it's a full fire. You can add wood to banked embers; it all counts.

> *Margin note:* a banked cauldron never steams. It's the overnight setting: warm water in the morning, embers still alive, and a third of the woodpile spent.

## 6. The Smokehouse

A plank shed two tiles long under a flat roof of boards, built of thick planks with a crack of light between some of them, its wide door on one end, with a rope handle under the latch (Build menu, Furniture: Woodwork 3, 14 planks, 20 nails, a hammer kept; 15 Carpentry XP). It stands in the open or under an open roof, never in a room, and the tile in front of its door stays clear. Inside it is always *In a shed* (chapter 8). It holds 6 stacks of whatever the TP mods smoke: today the fish, meat and sausage of *Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat)*, whose guide tells what each does in the smoke.

**The flue.** A Stone Hearth beside the shed, on its back or either flank (never with its door towards the shed), can have its smoke led in, by a **clay pipe** out of the stone's side low down that climbs a little into the shed's wall, or by a **trench** in the ground roofed with flat stones from its foot.

| To join a Stone Hearth | Takes | Tool, kept |
|---|---|---|
| **With a Clay Pipe** | 2 Clay Pipe Segments, 1 Clay | Mason's Trowel |
| **With a Stone Trench** (a longer, cooler run for the smoke) | 3 Flat Stones, 2 Clay | Shovel or Spade |

Right-click the Stone Hearth or the shed: **Connect the Stone Hearth**, about half a minute, 5 Masonry XP. Each way shows what it takes, the tool and anything you're missing. Not at the shed's door end, not with the Stone Hearth's door towards it, and not while the fire burns; one Stone Hearth a shed, and one thing joined to a Stone Hearth. **Disconnect the Stone Hearth** gives the pipe segments or stones back; the clay is lost. Neither can be picked up while they are joined.

Joined, the pipe or trench shows running into the shed's wall, the Stone Hearth's hole is covered (its lid, or the pot when stove food is in), and its smoke goes up the flue instead of out of the hole or round the door. Stone Hearth Info says *Flue: joined to the Smokehouse (clay pipe)*. A ham hangs by the door while finished stacks wait inside. You can still cook in the Stone Hearth while it smokes, and still fit the cauldron.

> *Margin note:* the Smokehouse used to belong to *Fish & Meat*. It moved here so cheese, and anything else a later mod smokes, can hang in the same shed over the same fire.

> *Margin note:* the low firebox outside the wall is how a Bosnian *pušnica* is fed today: the fire stays outside, where it can be tended and kept small, and only its smoke goes in to the meat. A long trench cools the smoke on the way. Older smokehouses, and the "black kitchens" of the Alps and Slovenia, simply hung the meat over the house's own open hearth.

## 7. By the Fire

A fire warms what stands beside it. Yeast's shelf, Vinegar's floor, vessel and cask, and Dairy's incubator and crock count two more places:

| Place | What makes it | Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| **By a fire** | Any lit fire within 2 tiles, on the same floor: a Stone Hearth, a fireplace, an antique stove, a brazier or a campfire. Never an electric or gas stove | **28 C** indoors |
| **By banked embers** | A banked Stone Hearth within 2 tiles, with no full fire in reach | **24 C** indoors |

**Indoors** (a room, a loft, a basement) the walls keep the fire's heat in, whatever the weather. **Outside** the air carries it off, so a fire there only warms the air it stands in, never past the indoor heat:

| Where the fire stands | Lit | Banked |
|---|---|---|
| Indoors | 28 C | 24 C |
| Under an open roof | the 10-day average **+15 C**, up to 28 | the 10-day average **+10 C**, up to 24 |
| In the open | the day's weather **+10 C**, up to 28 | the day's weather **+5 C**, up to 24 |

So a campfire in a January field (−4 C) holds a station at about 6 C, a porch fire about 11 C: a culture by either still works, only slower. In summer every fire reaches its full heat. The station's window says which: *By a fire*, *By a fire, under a roof*, *By a fire, in the open*.

> *Margin note:* a fire heats by radiation, and only on the side that faces it. Indoors the walls and the air between them warm up and stay warm; out of doors the wind takes the warm air as fast as the fire makes it. A shepherd by his fire on a winter night is warm in front and frosted at the back.

While you're away, the fire counts only for as long as its fuel would have lasted. After that, the station is back in whatever place it stands in without the fire.

## 8. Places and Weather

Every station in the series works by the temperature of **where it stands**. The weather is noted every 10 in-game minutes for the whole world, so a station you left a month ago has had exactly the weather it should have had when you come back.

**The station tells you.** A station's own window (Yeast's shelf, Vinegar's floor and vessel, Salt's vat, Fish & Meat's frames, tub and Smokehouse) lists its place, the temperature there and how fast it is working (Salt's vat, which minds no weather, the brine it will give), with a bar and the time left for each batch. A station without a crafting window (a Stone Hearth, Vinegar's cask, Dairy's incubator and crock) opens an Info window when you click it. Hover a running batch for the real time left at today's weather.

| Place | When | Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| **Open air** | Nothing overhead | The day's weather, hour by hour |
| **Under an open roof** | A roof or a floor overhead, but not a room: a porch, a lean-to, an open shed | The average outdoor temperature of the last 10 days |
| **Indoors** | A room: walls and a whole roof, built by you or found | The average outdoor temperature of the last 10 days, **2 C warmer in the cold months** (in full when that average is 10 C or below, less as it nears 18 C, none above); **22 C while the power grid is on** |
| **Loft** | The top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it | As indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on |
| **Basement** | Any level below ground | Follows the ground: the 10-day average, a month behind, and only two fifths as far from 10 C: about **5 C from January to March, 9 C in May, 13 C from July to September** |
| **In a shed** | Inside the Smokehouse: a closed plank shed standing out of doors | The day's weather, as the open air, out of the rain and wind; the power grid doesn't warm it |

> *Margin note:* four walls with no roof are still open air. A shed you build with walls and a whole roof is a room, as vanilla sees it. *In a shed* is the Smokehouse's own: a box of thin planks has no walls' worth of warmth to hold, so a frost at night reaches it by morning.

> *Margin note:* why ten days? Walls follow the season, not the afternoon. One warm day in January does nothing, but spring still arrives within a fortnight. An unheated house is nearly as cold as the season: in Kentucky's January that is still below freezing. It keeps a couple of degrees over the air in winter (its mass, the people in it, the sun through the glass), but no more: a house with no fire freezes the water in its pipes. Vanilla's rooms start from the outdoor temperature with the grid off.

> *Margin note:* a cellar runs a month behind the weather. It is coldest in March and warmest in August, so it is cool in spring when the house is warming and mild in autumn when the house is cold. In a new game the basement takes about a month to settle: half a degree warm in the first July.

| Month | Under an open roof (the 10-day average) | Indoors | Loft | Basement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | −4 C | −2 C | −2 C | 5 C |
| March | 1 C | 3 C | 3 C | 5 C |
| April | 6 C | 8 C | 8 C | 7 C |
| May | 13 C | 14 C | 15 C | 9 C |
| July | 21 C | 21 C | 24 C | 13 C |
| September | 15 C | 16 C | 17 C | 13 C |
| October | 9 C | 11 C | 11 C | 12 C |
| November | 3 C | 5 C | 5 C | 9 C |

*(The game's own sampled year, which runs colder than real Kentucky.)*

Each mod has its own idea of a good temperature, and its own **Temperature sensitivity** in the sandbox options:

| Setting | How much a bad temperature slows things |
|---|---|
| Off | Not at all |
| Forgiving | Half as much |
| Normal | As written in each guide |
| Harsh | Half as much again |

Rooms aren't heated by anything but the grid's power (22 C, as vanilla's rooms are) or a fire beside the station. Once the grid is off, a winter batch wants a fire.

## 9. Milk and Other Fluids That Go Off

In vanilla, no liquid ever goes bad. TP Core lets a mod name one that does, and keeps its age for it wherever it sits: a bucket, a bottle, the Forged Cauldron.

- **It ages by its place**, like a station: open air, under an open roof, indoors, in a loft or in a basement. A **powered fridge** counts as 4 C, and a **powered freezer** stops it.
- **Pouring never makes it young.** Mix two lots and the older age wins.
- **In the cauldron it ages too**, and a cauldron that has steamed for the mod's hold (Dairy: 20 minutes) scalds it.
- **Some can be cured.** A mod may let you stir something in to keep a fluid longer. Right-click the container: its own submenu (*Bucket*, beside the game's options) has the cure, one line for each thing you can use, saying how much of it you have; its note says what it takes, and a greyed line says why not. Cured fluid poured into cured stays cured; fresh poured in makes the lot fresh again.
- Dairy uses this for milk, and Fish & Meat for blood (cured with salt or vinegar). Without them, nothing ages. Their guides have the times.

**Seasoning afterwards.** A mod may let you season a food after it is made, as you would a stew (Fish & Meat's sausage links). Right-click it: its own submenu has **Season**, one line for each seasoning you have within reach that isn't in it yet, with the uses you have. Each goes in once, as many as you like, takes what a stew would take (a use; half an onion), and makes the food a little nicer to eat, as a spice does a stew: -2 unhappiness and -2 boredom. The seasonings show as small icons on its tooltip, and stay with it through what the mod does to it next. With several selected, each one that lacks it gets it.

**Within reach.** Whenever one of the series' recipes or menus counts salt, vinegar or a filler past what the recipe's own line takes (packing a big fish in salt, curing a bucket of blood, the oats in a black pudding), it counts what a craft can reach: your inventory and bags, and the containers and floor around you.

## 10. The Mason's and the Smith's Notebook

*Chalked on a slate by the kiln, half rubbed out.*

### What a Stone Hearth with its cauldron costs, and what it gives

| | |
|---|---|
| The stone | 8 Clay Bricks, 2 Clay; Masonry 2 |
| The cauldron, at a forge | 2 Steel Sheets, 3 Charcoal, 1 Wire; Blacksmith 4 |
| Hanging it | 2 Iron Pipes, 1 Heavy Chain, 1 Large Hook, all back when you take it down |
| Holds | 60 L |
| Wood for a full cauldron | about 2¾ hours of fire to the steam, 3¼ to boil water clean |
| Banked | the same wood lasts three times as long, the cauldron at 50 C |

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why iron, not copper?** Copper carries heat better, and the Alpine cheese kettles were copper; but a cauldron can be beaten from iron sheet at any farm forge, and greased and blacked it neither rusts nor taints the milk. Copper is kept for the cheese vat.
- **Why doesn't milk boil?** The dairymaid kept it off the boil: milk is scalded at about 85 C, not boiled, or it catches and skins over. The cauldron holds it at 95 C at most. Water is another matter: it was boiled in the copper for the wash, the brew and the scalding of pails.
- **Why 60 L?** Real farmhouse coppers and set kettles held 40 to 100 L (ten to twenty gallons). Sixty is the middle of the range: a wash day's water, or a whole morning's milking.
- **Why hang it from a chain?** Swing it off the fire and it stops heating; drop it on the hook and the fire's whole heat goes into it. Cheesemakers turned theirs on a wooden crane for just that.
- **Why bank a fire?** Ash keeps the air off the coals, so they glow instead of flaming. A banked fire could be woken with a poker in the morning, which mattered when the alternative was flint and tinder.
- **Why does a cellar swing so little, and run late?** A few feet of earth take the edge off the seasons and slow them down: a cellar runs two fifths of the way from the ground's mean towards the season, a month or so behind it, cool in May and above frost in March. That's why cheese, beer and roots were kept below ground.

### Old names, old ways

- **The copper.** In an old farmhouse "the copper" was the big boiling pan built into brickwork over its own small firebox, often in the scullery. Monday's wash, the brewing, the pig's mash and the Christmas puddings all went through it, whatever metal it was made of.
- **Set pots.** The same thing in a dairy or a dye-house was a "set pot" or a "furnace pan": a vessel set in masonry so the fire's heat wrapped round it instead of escaping.
- **Curfew.** The word comes from the French *couvre-feu*, "cover the fire": the evening bell that told a town to bank its hearths for the night.

## 11. Tips

- **Fill it up.** A full 60 L cauldron takes only about an hour and a quarter longer to steam than 10 L.
- **Boil your water by the cauldronful.** A rain barrel's tainted water, boiled clean 60 L at a time, saves a stove a whole afternoon of pots.
- **Bank it overnight.** A third of the wood, and anything beside it still stands at 24 C. The rest of the room cools, so sleep near it.
- **Build the stations that need it within 2 tiles.** Dairy's incubator and crock pour straight from the cauldron at that range, and sit "by a fire" at the same time.
- **Check Stone Hearth Info before you walk off.** If the fuel won't last until the steam, it says "Not yet".
- **One fire, two jobs.** A Stone Hearth joined to a Smokehouse can carry the cauldron too: render fat or scald milk while the hams smoke.
- **Empty it before you move it.** The cauldron comes off only when it's empty and the fire is out, and the Stone Hearth moves only bare.

## 12. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| No Forge Cauldron recipe | You haven't learned it: read *Everyday Smithing - May 1993*, reach Blacksmith 5 with Cooking 2, or research a cauldron |
| Fit Cauldron is greyed out | The fire is lit, or you're missing a piece (its note says which). Pliers are kept |
| Remove Cauldron is greyed out | The fire is lit, or the cauldron holds something: Empty the Cauldron first |
| Pour into the Cauldron is greyed out | The cauldron is full, or you have no container with a fluid in it |
| A container shows in red | It holds a mixture, or a fluid that doesn't go with what's in the cauldron. It takes one fluid at a time |
| Fill from the Cauldron is greyed out | You need an empty container, or one holding the same fluid with room left |
| Steaming says "Not yet (85 C)" | The fire is out, or its fuel won't last until the cauldron steams. Add wood |
| Steaming says "Not while banked" | A banked fire holds 50 C. Stoke it |
| No Bank the Fire option | The fire isn't lit |
| No Light Fire option on a Stone Hearth that looks out | It's banked: Stoke the Fire, or Put out Fire first |
| Can't pick the Stone Hearth up | It's burning, a cauldron is fitted, or it's joined to a Smokehouse |
| Connect the Stone Hearth is greyed out | Its note says why: the fire burns, its door faces the shed, it's at the shed's door end, or one of them is already joined |
| A Stone Hearth or Smokehouse won't build or go down there | Something stands in front of its door (or it would stand in front of another one's), or a wall is on that edge. Turn it, or clear the tile. `TPC.doors()` names what is in the way |
| The Stone Hearth or another TP station is missing | Check `~/Zomboid/console.txt`. The tile number 5173 must be unique among your mods |

## 13. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPC.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPC.speed(x)` | Game speed: the clock runs `x` times as fast (`getGameTime():setMultiplier(x)`); `TPC.speed()` shows it. Vanilla's speed buttons are 1, 5, 20 and 40, and clicking one sets vanilla's speed again. |
| `TPC.kit(kind)` | Gives what a station takes: `"hearth"` (the default: the Stone Hearth's build and Fit Cauldron's kit), `"firebox"` (a mason's trowel, 8 clay bricks and 2 clay, Masonry 2), `"cauldron"` (a Forged Cauldron, 2 iron pipes, a heavy chain, a large hook and pliers), `"forge"` (Forge Cauldron's materials and tools, Blacksmith 4, and the magazine), `"smokehouse"` (a hammer, 14 planks and 20 nails, Woodwork 3), `"flue"` (both flue kits: 2 Clay Pipe Segments, 3 Flat Stones, 3 Clay, with a Mason's Trowel and a Shovel) or `"hole"` (a stove food, an oven food and a pot to fill, for the hole's looks). `TPC.kit("?")` lists them. |
| `TPC.weather()` | The series' weather as every TP mod reads it: the temperature now, the season's mean and humidity, the ground's mean (the season a month behind, which a basement follows), and each place's temperature. |
| `TPC.here()` | Which place you're standing in, and its temperature. |
| `TPC.setWeather(c, season, ground)` | Pretends the weather for a test that wants a cold month or a warm day: the day's temperature `c` (the engine's own override, the one the debug menu's Climate debugger sets), the season's mean (`c` if left out) and the ground's (left alone if left out). The mods read it at their next ten-minute reading, so run the clock; `TPC.weather()` shows it then. The season drifts towards the day again as it would. `TPC.setWeather()` ends it. Not kept through a load. |
| `TPC.fit()` | Fits the Cauldron on the nearest Stone Hearth now, no items taken; `TPC.fit(false)` takes off what is fitted, nothing back. |
| `TPC.cauldronInfo()` | The nearest Stone Hearth with the cauldron within 2 tiles: position, fire (lit, banked or out), fuel in minutes, sprite, the cauldron's litres, fluid and heat, when it will steam, and until when the fire burns. |
| `TPC.cauldron(litres, c, fluid)` | Empties its cauldron and fills it: `litres` (0 leaves it empty) of `fluid` at `c` degrees. `TPC.cauldron()` is 10 L of CowMilk at 20 C; it holds 60. |
| `TPC.fuel(minutes)` | Sets the fuel of the nearest Stone Hearth, fitted or not (600, ten hours, if left out); banked, what the embers have left. Vanilla's menu still lights it. |
| `TPC.warmth()` | Air C and light 1-6 tiles east of the nearest Stone Hearth (Cauldron) and on your square, your core heat, the firebox's heat (and its custom heat while banked), and each cookable food's heat and cooking minutes. |
| `TPC.hot(minutes)` | Brings its cauldron to the steam (90 C) now, counted as having steamed that long (an hour if left out, past Dairy's 20-minute scald). `TPC.hot(0)` lets you watch a hold count up. |
| `TPC.stone()` | The nearest Stone Hearth within 2 tiles, fitted or not: position, fire, fuel in minutes, sprite, what is fitted, what is joined (its kit and side), the hole's look, and the square on its back and each flank. |
| `TPC.doors()` | The tile you stand on: whether a door facing S or E could open there (or what blocks it: a wall edge or an object) and whether a Stone Hearth's or Smokehouse's door opens onto it. |
| `TPC.smokehouse()` | Each Smokehouse tile within 3 tiles on your floor: its facing, back or door tile, the entry overlay it shows, and each Stone Hearth beside it with its flue and why it can't join (or `may join`, `joined here`). |
| `TPC.flue(kind, side)` | Joins the Smokehouse's flue to the nearest Stone Hearth at once, for an art check: `kind` Pipe or Stones, `side` Back, Left or Right (Back if left out); `TPC.flue()` caps it. |

**Typical check of the cauldron:** `TPC.kit()`, build a Stone Hearth, Fit Cauldron (or `TPC.fit()`), `TPC.cauldron(10)`, `TPC.fuel()`, light it from vanilla's menu, and watch Stone Hearth Info count down to the steam (about 1.5 hours). `TPC.hot()` skips the wait.

**Typical check of the hole:** `TPC.kit("firebox")` and `TPC.kit("hole")`, build one, `TPC.fuel()`, light it: smoke rises out of the open hole. Put the pie in: the lid, and the smoke leaks round the door. Add the stew: the lidded pot.

**Typical check of banking:** with the fire lit and `TPC.cauldron(10, 90)`, Bank the Fire. Vanilla's flames go out and the door's vents show dull embers; Stone Hearth Info reads Banked, the fuel time triples, and the cauldron settles towards 50 C. `TPC.warmth()` shows the air warm only within 2 tiles.
