## 1. What This Mod Does

**Traditional Preservation (Core)**, TP Core for short, is the mod every Traditional Preservation mod needs: Salt, Fish & Meat, Yeast, Vinegar and Dairy. It carries the rules they share, so they all agree about the weather, the cellar and the time you were away.

It also adds two stations of its own, shared by the series: the **Hearth**, a stone fireplace with a 60 L copper in its top for heating whatever you pour into it, and the **Firebox**, the same fire without the copper (chapter 6).

On its own it adds only those two, and changes no vanilla recipe, item or loot.

---

## 2. Quick Start

> Enable **Traditional Preservation (Core)** with any Traditional Preservation mod. It loads first.

| Step | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Build a **Hearth** (Build menu, Masonry) | 12 Clay Bricks, 4 Clay, 1 Copper Sheet, a mason's trowel (kept). Masonry 2 | A fireplace with a 60 L copper. 10 Masonry XP |
| **Add Fuel** and **Light** it | Firewood and a lighter, as any fireplace | Fire, room heat, a firebox that cooks |
| **Pour into the Copper** | Any one fluid, up to 60 L | The copper heating towards 95 C |
| Wait | About 1.5 hours for 10 L | The copper **steaming** at 85 C |
| **Fill from the Copper** | Empty containers | Your fluid back, hot |
| With Fish & Meat: **Put Fat in the Copper** | Raw fat from butchering | Lard (Melted) or Tallow after an hour of steaming; it never burns here |

## 3. The Hearth

A waist-high block of split fieldstone with a lime-rendered top, an arched fire mouth on the side it faces, and a hammered copper pan set in it. It has two facings.

To the game it is a fireplace, so everything a fireplace does, it does: add fuel, light it, put it out, cook in its firebox, warm the room. The copper is the new part.

Right-click it. In its **Hearth** submenu you'll find:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| *Copper: 10 / 60 L Cow's Milk (Fresh), 62 C* | A grey status line: what the copper holds and how hot it is |
| **Hearth Info** | A small window: the fire, the fuel, the copper, and when it will steam. **Clicking the Hearth opens it too** |
| **Bank the Fire** / **Stoke the Fire** | While the fire is lit; also a button beside Put out Fire in the loot window. See chapter 5 |
| **Pour into the Copper** | From any container on you or nearby with a fluid in it |
| **Fill from the Copper** | Into empty containers, or ones holding the same fluid with room left |
| **Empty the Copper** | Pours away everything in it at once: for rotten milk, or a fluid you're done with. Longer the more there is |

**Hearth Info** shows:

| Line | Reads |
|---|---|
| Fire | Lit, Banked or Out |
| Fuel | A bar (full is 12 hours of wood) and the time it will burn |
| Copper | A bar and the litres, of 60 |
| Holds | The fluid, or each fluid of a blend |
| Heat | Its temperature |
| Steaming | "Yes", "in 1.2 hours", "Not yet (85 C)" or "Not while banked" |

The window updates while it's open, and closes when you walk more than 6 squares away.

**You can't pick a Hearth up** while its fire burns or its copper holds anything.

**Keep the mouth clear.** The tile in front of the Hearth's fire mouth must be free: it won't build or go down with a crate, a fence, a wall on that edge or another station there, and nothing of the Traditional Preservation mods can be built on that tile afterwards. Picked up empty, it turns between its two facings as you put it down.

## 4. The Copper

- **60 L, one fluid at a time.** Any fluid: water, milk, whatever a container holds. A mixture is refused, and so is a fluid that doesn't go with what's already in the copper. Both show in red, with the reason.
- **Some fluids may share it.** A mod can say which: Dairy lets cow's, sheep's and animal's milk mix, and the blend keeps its shares in and out.
- **Heat mixes by volume.** Cold poured onto hot cools the lot.
- **Lit:** it heats towards 95 C. Milk and the rest are kept off the boil.
- **Water boils.** Water on its own comes to a rolling boil at 100 C. Tainted water held there for **10 minutes** comes out clean: a whole copper of rain-barrel water made safe at once. If the fire goes out or you pour more in, the 10 minutes start over.
- **At 85 C it steams.** You see it in the copper, and in Hearth Info.
- **Fire out:** it cools towards the temperature of the place the Hearth stands in. Slowly: the stone holds its heat.
- **More takes longer**, both ways.

| In the copper | From 20 C to the steam, fire lit | From 85 C down to 45 C, fire out, 20 C room |
|---|---|---|
| 5 L | about 1¼ hours | about 1½ hours |
| 10 L | about 1½ hours | about 2 hours |
| 20 L | about 2 hours | about 2½ hours |
| 60 L (full) | about 2¾ hours | about 3½ hours |

| Water | From 20 C to clean (boiling, then 10 minutes) |
|---|---|
| 10 L | about 1¾ hours |
| 20 L | about 2¼ hours |
| 60 L (full) | about 3¼ hours |

Water steams sooner than milk: nobody holds it back from the boil.

> *Margin note:* six times the fluid doesn't take six times the wait. A bigger potful has less surface for its size, so the time grows with the cube root of the volume: a full 60 L copper takes less than twice as long as 10 L.

What you fill from the copper leaves at the copper's heat. An ordinary container in your hands is back at room heat within seconds, as vanilla has it. Some mods keep the heat for you: Dairy's milk remembers it, and its Wrapped Yogurt Pot holds it for hours.

**While you're away** the copper carries on: it heats for as long as the fuel it had would have lasted, then cools.

## 5. Banking the Fire

**Bank the Fire** rakes a lit fire down under its own ash. The flames go out and the mouth shows dull embers.

| | Lit | Banked |
|---|---|---|
| Wood burnt | all of it | **a third** |
| The same fuel lasts | 1× | **3×** |
| The copper | heats towards 95 C | settles at about **50 C** |
| The firebox cooks | at full speed | a third as fast |
| Warms the room | 8 tiles round | only the **2 tiles** round it |
| Light | the whole room | a faint glow |
| Stations within 2 tiles (chapter 7) | 28 C | 24 C |

**Stoke the Fire** rakes the embers back up into a full fire.

The embers keep burning while you're away, at their third. **Put out Fire** works on a banked fire too, and a banked fire that burns down goes out. Either way it isn't banked any more: light it again and it's a full fire. You can add wood to banked embers; it all counts.

> *Margin note:* a banked copper never steams. It's the overnight setting: warm water in the morning, embers still alive, and a third of the woodpile spent.

## 6. The Firebox

The Hearth without its copper: the same fieldstone and lime top, built knee high, with an **iron door** over the fire mouth. You see the fire only through the door's vents, three draught holes low down and two slits under the arch.

| | Hearth | Firebox |
|---|---|---|
| Build (Masonry 2, trowel kept) | 12 Clay Bricks, 4 Clay, 1 Copper Sheet | **8 Clay Bricks, 2 Clay** |
| Masonry XP | 10 | 8 |
| Copper | 60 L | none |
| Firebox cooks like an oven | yes | yes |
| Warms stations within 2 tiles | yes | yes |
| Bank the Fire / Stoke the Fire | yes | yes |
| Info window | Hearth Info | Firebox Info: fire, fuel, a note |
| Flue | its own | capped, or joined to a building beside it |

**The door.** As with the Hearth, the tile in front of the Firebox's door must stay free, and a picked-up Firebox turns as you put it down. It can't be picked up while it burns, or while its flue is joined to a building.

**The flue.** On its own the flue is capped with a flat stone, and while it burns the smoke leaks out round the door. A mod can lead it into a building on the Firebox's back or either flank (never the door's side): the Smokehouse of *Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat)* does, with a **clay pipe** from the top or a **trench** in the ground roofed with flat stones from its foot. The Firebox then shows that flue, and the smoke goes where it should.

> *Margin note:* the low firebox outside the wall is how a Bosnian *pušnica* is fed today: the fire stays outside, where it can be tended and kept small, and only its smoke goes in to the meat. A long trench cools the smoke on the way. Older smokehouses, and the "black kitchens" of the Alps and Slovenia, simply hung the meat over the house's own open hearth.

## 7. By the Fire

A fire warms what stands beside it. Yeast's shelf, Vinegar's floor, vessel and cask, and Dairy's incubator and crock count two more places:

| Place | What makes it | Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| **By a fire** | Any lit fire within 2 tiles, on the same floor: a Hearth, a Firebox, a fireplace, an antique stove, a brazier or a campfire. Never an electric or gas stove | **28 C** |
| **By banked embers** | A banked Hearth or Firebox within 2 tiles, with no full fire in reach | **24 C** |

While you're away, the fire counts only for as long as its fuel would have lasted. After that, the station is back in whatever place it stands in without the fire.

## 8. Places and Weather

Every station in the series works by the temperature of **where it stands**. The weather is noted every 10 in-game minutes for the whole world, so a station you left a month ago has had exactly the weather it should have had when you come back.

| Place | When | Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| **Open air** | Nothing overhead | The day's weather, hour by hour |
| **Under an open roof** | A roof or a floor overhead, but not a room: a porch, a lean-to, an open shed | The average outdoor temperature of the last 10 days |
| **Indoors** | A room: walls and a whole roof, built by you or found | The average outdoor temperature of the last 10 days; **22 C while the power grid is on** |
| **Loft** | The top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it | As indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on |
| **Basement** | Any level below ground | Follows the ground: the 10-day average, a month behind, and only two fifths as far from 10 C: about **5 C from January to March, 9 C in May, 13 C from July to September** |

> *Margin note:* four walls with no roof are still open air. A shed you build with walls and a whole roof is a room, as vanilla sees it.

> *Margin note:* why ten days? Walls follow the season, not the afternoon. One warm day in January does nothing, but spring still arrives within a fortnight. An unheated house is as cold as the season: in Kentucky's January that is below freezing. Vanilla agrees: with the grid off, its rooms start from the outdoor temperature.

> *Margin note:* a cellar runs a month behind the weather. It is coldest in March and warmest in August, so it is cool in spring when the house is warming and mild in autumn when the house is cold. In a new game the basement takes about a month to settle: half a degree warm in the first July.

| Month | Open air and indoors (the 10-day average) | Loft | Basement |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | −4 C | −4 C | 5 C |
| March | 1 C | 1 C | 5 C |
| April | 6 C | 6 C | 7 C |
| May | 13 C | 14 C | 9 C |
| July | 21 C | 24 C | 13 C |
| September | 15 C | 16 C | 13 C |
| October | 9 C | 9 C | 12 C |
| November | 3 C | 3 C | 9 C |

*(The game's own sampled year, which runs colder than real Kentucky.)*

Each mod has its own idea of a good temperature, and its own **Temperature sensitivity** in the sandbox options:

| Setting | How much a bad temperature slows things |
|---|---|
| Off | Not at all |
| Forgiving | Half as much |
| Normal | As written in each guide |
| Harsh | Half as much again |

Rooms aren't heated by anything but the grid's power (22 C, as vanilla's rooms are) or a fire beside the station. Once the grid is off, a winter batch wants a fire.

## 9. Milk and Other Fluids That Go Off

In vanilla, no liquid ever goes bad. TP Core lets a mod name one that does, and keeps its age for it wherever it sits: a bucket, a bottle, the Hearth's copper.

- **It ages by its place**, like a station: open air, under an open roof, indoors, in a loft or in a basement. A **powered fridge** counts as 4 C, and a **powered freezer** stops it.
- **Pouring never makes it young.** Mix two lots and the older age wins.
- **In the copper it ages too**, and a steaming copper scalds it.
- Today only Dairy uses this, for milk. Without Dairy, nothing ages. Its guide has the times.

## 10. The Mason's Notebook

*Chalked on a slate by the kiln, half rubbed out.*

### What a Hearth costs, and what it gives

| | |
|---|---|
| Bricks and clay | 12 Clay Bricks, 4 Clay |
| Metal | 1 Copper Sheet |
| Skill | Masonry 2 |
| Holds | 60 L |
| Wood for a full copper | about 2¾ hours of fire to the steam, 3¼ to boil water clean |
| Banked | the same wood lasts three times as long, the copper at 50 C |

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why copper?** It carries heat better than iron and far better than clay, so the fire's heat reaches the whole potful, and it doesn't taint milk or wort.
- **Why doesn't milk boil?** The dairymaid kept it off the boil: milk is scalded at about 85 C, not boiled, or it catches and skins over. The copper holds it at 95 C at most. Water is another matter: it was boiled in the copper for the wash, the brew and the scalding of pails.
- **Why 60 L?** Real farmhouse coppers held 40 to 100 L (ten to twenty gallons). Sixty is the middle of the range: a wash day's water, or a whole morning's milking.
- **Why bank a fire?** Ash keeps the air off the coals, so they glow instead of flaming. A banked fire could be woken with a poker in the morning, which mattered when the alternative was flint and tinder.
- **Why does a cellar swing so little, and run late?** A few feet of earth take the edge off the seasons and slow them down: a cellar runs two fifths of the way from the ground's mean towards the season, a month or so behind it, cool in May and above frost in March. That's why cheese, beer and roots were kept below ground.

### Old names, old ways

- **The copper.** In an old farmhouse "the copper" was the big boiling pan built into brickwork over its own small firebox, often in the scullery. Monday's wash, the brewing, the pig's mash and the Christmas puddings all went through it.
- **Set pots.** The same thing in a dairy or a dye-house was a "set pot" or a "furnace pan": a vessel set in masonry so the fire's heat wrapped round it instead of escaping.
- **Curfew.** The word comes from the French *couvre-feu*, "cover the fire": the evening bell that told a town to bank its hearths for the night.

## 11. Tips

- **Fill it up.** A full 60 L copper takes only about an hour and a quarter longer to steam than 10 L.
- **Boil your water by the copperful.** A rain barrel's tainted water, boiled clean 60 L at a time, saves a stove a whole afternoon of pots.
- **Bank it overnight.** A third of the wood, and anything beside it still stands at 24 C. The rest of the room cools, so sleep near it.
- **Build the stations that need it within 2 tiles.** Dairy's incubator and crock pour straight from the copper at that range, and sit "by a fire" at the same time.
- **Check Hearth Info before you walk off.** If the fuel won't last until the steam, it says "Not yet".
- **Empty it before you move it.** The Hearth stays put while it's lit or holds a drop.

## 12. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Pour into the Copper is greyed out | The copper is full, or you have no container with a fluid in it |
| A container shows in red | It holds a mixture, or a fluid that doesn't go with what's in the copper. The copper takes one fluid at a time |
| Fill from the Copper is greyed out | You need an empty container, or one holding the same fluid with room left |
| Steaming says "Not yet (85 C)" | The fire is out, or its fuel won't last until the copper steams. Add wood |
| Steaming says "Not while banked" | A banked fire holds 50 C. Stoke it |
| No Bank the Fire option | The fire isn't lit |
| No Light Fire option on a Hearth that looks out | It's banked: Stoke the Fire, or Put out Fire first |
| Can't pick the Hearth up | It's lit, or the copper isn't empty |
| A Hearth or Firebox won't build or go down there | Something stands in front of its mouth or door (or it would stand in front of another one's), or a wall is on that edge. Turn it, or clear the tile. `TPC.doors()` names what is in the way |
| The Hearth or another TP station is missing | Check `~/Zomboid/console.txt`. The tile number 5173 must be unique among your mods |

## 13. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPC.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPC.speed(x)` | Game speed: the clock runs `x` times as fast (`getGameTime():setMultiplier(x)`); `TPC.speed()` shows it. Vanilla's speed buttons are 1, 5, 20 and 40, and clicking one sets vanilla's speed again. |
| `TPC.kit(kind)` | Gives what a station takes to build: `"hearth"` (the default: a mason's trowel, 12 clay bricks, 4 clay and a copper sheet) or `"firebox"` (a trowel, 8 clay bricks and 2 clay); Masonry 2 for both. `TPC.kit("?")` lists them. |
| `TPC.weather()` | The series' weather as every TP mod reads it: the temperature now, the season's mean and humidity, the ground's mean (the season a month behind, which a basement follows), and each place's temperature. |
| `TPC.here()` | Which place you're standing in, and its temperature. |
| `TPC.setWeather(c, season, ground)` | Pretends the weather for a test that wants a cold month or a warm day: the day's temperature `c` (the engine's own override, the one the debug menu's Climate debugger sets), the season's mean (`c` if left out) and the ground's (left alone if left out). The mods read it at their next ten-minute reading, so run the clock; `TPC.weather()` shows it then. The season drifts towards the day again as it would. `TPC.setWeather()` ends it. Not kept through a load. |
| `TPC.hearth()` | The nearest Hearth within 2 tiles: position, fire (lit, banked or out), fuel in minutes, sprite, the copper's litres, fluid and heat, when it will steam, and until when the fire burns. |
| `TPC.copper(litres, c, fluid)` | Empties its copper and fills it: `litres` (0 leaves it empty) of `fluid` at `c` degrees. `TPC.copper()` is 10 L of CowMilk at 20 C; the copper holds 60. |
| `TPC.fuel(minutes)` | Sets the fuel of the nearest Hearth or Firebox (600, ten hours, if left out); banked, what the embers have left. Vanilla's menu still lights it. |
| `TPC.warmth()` | Air C and light 1-6 tiles east of the nearest Hearth and on your square, your core heat, the firebox's heat (and its custom heat while banked), and each cookable food's heat and cooking minutes. |
| `TPC.hot()` | Brings its copper to the steam (90 C) now. |
| `TPC.firebox()` | The nearest Firebox within 2 tiles: position, fire, fuel in minutes, sprite, its flue and side, and the square on each of its flue's sides. |
| `TPC.doors()` | The tile you stand on: whether a door facing S or E could open there (or what blocks it: a wall edge or an object) and whether a Hearth's, Firebox's or Smokehouse's door opens onto it. |
| `TPC.flue(kind, side)` | Shows its flue for an art check: `kind` Pipe or Stones, `side` Back, Left or Right (Back if left out); `TPC.flue()` caps it. The Smokehouse joins it for real. |

**Typical check of the copper:** `TPC.kit()`, build a Hearth, `TPC.copper(10)`, `TPC.fuel()`, light it from vanilla's menu, and watch Hearth Info count down to the steam (about 1.5 hours). `TPC.hot()` skips the wait.

**Typical check of the Firebox:** `TPC.kit("firebox")`, build one, `TPC.fuel()`, light it: the glow shows through the door's vents and smoke leaks round the door. `TPC.flue("Pipe", "Left")` shows a joined flue and the smoke stops.

**Typical check of banking:** with the fire lit and `TPC.copper(10, 90)`, Bank the Fire. Vanilla's flames go out and the mouth shows dull embers; Hearth Info reads Banked, the fuel time triples, and the copper settles towards 50 C. `TPC.warmth()` shows the air warm only within 2 tiles.
