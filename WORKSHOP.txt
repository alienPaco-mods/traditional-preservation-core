[h1]Traditional Preservation (Core)[/h1]

The shared mod of the Traditional Preservation series. Every Traditional Preservation mod (Salt, Fish, Yeast, Vinegar, Dairy) requires it. It holds what they share, and three stations of its own: the Hearth, the Firebox and the Smokehouse.

[b]Build 42 only (42.22 or newer). This is an early release, so back up your save before enabling it. Singleplayer only: multiplayer is not supported.[/b]

[h2]What it adds[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]The Hearth[/b]: a 60 L copper kettle hung from an iron gallows over a low stone firebox, for heating any fluid
[*][b]The Firebox[/b]: a low stone firebox with an iron door, for heat and cooking
[*][b]The Smokehouse[/b]: a plank shed two tiles long, joined to a Firebox by a clay pipe or a stone trench, where the TP mods smoke their food
[*]One weather record for the whole series, so every station keeps working while you are away
[*]Eight kinds of place a station can stand in: open air, under an open roof, indoors, a loft, a basement, in a shed, by a fire and by banked embers
[*]The popups and info windows that show a station's progress, real time left, temperature and working rate
[*]A [b]Season[/b] menu for foods the other mods let you season afterwards, as a stew (Fish & Meat's sausages): garlic, onion, pepper or herbs, each a little nicer to eat
[/list]

[h2]The Hearth[/h2]
Build menu, Masonry. Masonry 2: 8 Clay Bricks, 2 Clay, 2 Copper Sheets, 3 Iron Bars (or Metal Bars, Metal Pipes, Steel Bars), 1 Large Hook and a mason's trowel (kept).

It is a working fireplace: add fuel and light it as you would any other, cook in its firebox, warm the room. Its right-click menu also has:
[list]
[*][b]Pour into the Copper[/b]: up to 60 L of one fluid, from any container.
[*][b]Fill from the Copper[/b]: into your own containers, at the copper's heat.
[*][b]Empty the Copper[/b]: pour the whole lot away, rotten milk and all.
[*][b]Bank the Fire[/b]: the fire burns a third of the wood, holds the copper at about 50 C, warms only the 2 tiles round it and cooks a third as fast. [b]Stoke the Fire[/b] brings it back.
[*][b]Hearth Info[/b] (or just click the Hearth): the fire, the fuel and how long it will burn, the copper's contents and heat, and when it will steam.
[/list]

With the fire lit the copper heats towards 95 C and steams at 85 C: about 1.5 hours for 10 L, about 2.75 for a full 60 L. Water alone is let boil: tainted water held at the boil for 10 minutes comes out clean (about 1.75 hours for 10 L, 3.25 for a full copper). With the fire out it cools slowly, as the stone holds its heat. The copper shows empty, full or steaming, and the fire mouth glows.

A Hearth can't be picked up while its fire burns or its copper holds anything.

[h2]The Firebox[/h2]
Build menu, Masonry. Masonry 2: 8 Clay Bricks, 2 Clay and a mason's trowel (kept).

The Hearth's fire without the copper: knee high, with an iron door whose vents show the fire. It lights, cooks and warms like the Hearth, and banks and stokes the same way ([b]Firebox Info[/b] shows the fire and fuel). Its flue is capped on its own, so smoke leaks round the door; joined to a Smokehouse, the smoke goes into the shed. It can't be picked up while it burns or while its flue is joined.

[h2]The Smokehouse[/h2]

A plank shed two tiles long under one roof slope, its door on the high end (Woodwork 3: 14 planks, 20 nails, hammer kept). Open air or under an open roof, never in a room; 6 stacks. Right-click it or a Firebox beside it to [b]Connect the Firebox[/b] with a clay pipe (2 Clay Pipe Segments, 1 Clay, a Mason's Trowel kept) or a stone trench (3 Flat Stones, 2 Clay, a shovel kept). What hangs inside and what the smoke does to it is each mod's: Fish & Meat smokes fish, meat and sausage there.

The tile in front of the Hearth's mouth and the Firebox's door must stay clear: neither builds or goes down with something in front of it. Picked up, both turn to either facing as you put them down.

[h2]Places[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Open air[/b]: the day's weather.
[*][b]Under an open roof[/b] (a porch, an open shed) and [b]indoors[/b] (a room): the last 10 days' average outdoors, a room 2 C warmer in the cold months. A room is 22 C while the power grid is on.
[*][b]In a loft[/b] (the top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it): as indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on.
[*][b]In a basement[/b]: follows the ground, a month behind the season and two fifths as far: about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September.
[*][b]In a shed[/b] (inside the Smokehouse, a closed plank shed out of doors): the day's weather, as the open air, out of the rain and wind; the power grid doesn't warm it.
[/list]
Yeast, Vinegar and Dairy also count [b]by a fire[/b]: any lit fireplace, antique stove, campfire or Hearth within 2 tiles holds 28 C indoors, and banked embers (a Hearth's or a Firebox's) 24 C. Outside, the air carries the heat off: under an open roof a fire adds 15 C to the 10-day average (embers 10), in the open 10 C to the day's weather (embers 5), never past 28 and 24.
